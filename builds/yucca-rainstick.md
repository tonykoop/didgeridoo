# Yucca Rainstick Build Variant

Exploits yucca's built-in pith baffle to produce a rainstick without the spine-pin drilling step required in traditional cactus-cactus construction. The pith acts as a natural cascade obstruction: fill media tumbles through the partially-cleared pith channels, producing the characteristic rainfall sound.

> **Dust safety:** Yucca boring and sanding releases calcium-oxalate raphides. Wear a P100/N95 respirator, safety glasses, and long sleeves throughout. See `safety/agave-dust.md`.

---

## 1. How Yucca's Pith Works as a Baffle

In a cactus rainstick, thorns or dowels are inserted through the wall to create cascade obstructions inside a hollow tube. Yucca's interior pith is already a matrix of fibrous channels — partially clearing the pith creates the same obstruction pattern without drilling through the wall.

The goal is a **partial pith removal**: clear a central channel for fill-media flow, but leave 40–60% of the pith matrix intact as the baffle. Do not fully gouge the pith; that turns it into a hollow tube and kills the cascade effect.

## 2. Materials List

| Item | Spec / Notes |
|---|---|
| Yucca stalk section | Cured (pass `stock-prep/cure-check.md`); 24–60 in length; 2–4 in OD |
| Fill media (choose one or mix) | Small seeds (amaranth, rice, millet), dried pebbles (3–5 mm), glass beads |
| End-cap material | Cork stoppers, hardwood discs (1/4-in ply), or carved yucca plugs |
| Adhesive for end caps | Titebond III or 5-min epoxy |
| Finish (optional) | Boiled linseed oil, paste wax, or acrylic spray — protects outer cortex |
| Sandpaper | 80-grit and 150-grit for end faces and outer surface |
| Tools | Drill or hand borer, long drill bit or rebar (~1/4 in) for channel clearance, spoon gouge, flashlight for bore inspection |

## 3. Step-by-Step Procedure

### Step 1 — Select and Inspect the Stalk Section

1. Choose a cured yucca section 24–60 in long (see `stock-prep/cut-plan.md` — upper zone sections are ideal).
2. Confirm the section passes the cure-check: knuckle-tap rings clear end-to-end.
3. Check that the outer wall is structurally sound (no through-wall cracks).

### Step 2 — Square the Ends

1. Cross-cut both ends clean and square with a fine-tooth saw.
2. Sand the cut faces flat (80-grit, then 150-grit).
3. The end faces are where the caps will seat — they must be flat for a good seal.

### Step 3 — Partial Pith Clearance

1. From one end, use a long drill bit or a sharpened rebar rod to bore a central channel through the pith. Target: 3/4 – 1 in diameter channel down the centre axis.
2. Work in short passes, clearing debris frequently. Use a flashlight from the other end to gauge progress.
3. **Stop clearing when the channel is open end-to-end but the surrounding pith matrix is still present.** The pith should be visible as an irregular ring around the central channel when you look through it.
4. Shake the tube vertically — loose pith debris will fall out. Blow out dust.
5. Inspect with a flashlight: you should see the central channel flanked by pith baffle material on all sides.

> **Tip:** The amount of pith left in determines the cascade speed. More pith = slower, denser sound. Less pith = faster, lighter sound. Aim for ~50% of the pith cross-section intact on the first build.

### Step 4 — Test the Cascade (Dry Run)

1. Temporarily cap one end with your palm.
2. Add a small handful of fill media through the open end.
3. Tip the tube end-to-end slowly and listen. You should hear a steady, gradual cascade — not a single thump (too much pith cleared) and not silence (channel blocked).
4. Adjust pith clearance if needed:
   - Sound too abrupt → re-insert the borer and clear slightly more pith
   - No sound / no flow → the central channel is blocked; clear the obstruction

### Step 5 — Load Fill Media

1. Seal one end permanently (see Step 6) before loading.
2. Through the open end, pour in fill media. Standard fill: 2–4 tablespoons per foot of tube length as a starting point.
3. Adjust by ear: tip the tube and listen. Add more for a denser cascade, remove for a lighter one.

### Step 6 — End Caps

**Option A — Cork stoppers:** Select cork(s) that fit snugly in the bore opening. Dry-fit, then seat with a few drops of CA glue or epoxy around the perimeter. Let cure 24 h.

**Option B — Hardwood disc cap:** Cut a 1/4-in hardwood or ply disc to match the end OD. Glue and clamp with 5-min epoxy. Optionally add a recessed groove and ring of CA for a hermetic seal.

**Option C — Carved yucca plug:** Cut a short yucca section (2–3 in) slightly wider than the bore opening, carve to fit, and glue. Matches the look of the instrument body.

Seal both ends completely. Shake vigorously after curing to confirm no rattle from loose end-cap joints.

### Step 7 — Finish the Exterior (Optional)

1. Sand the outer cortex: 80-grit to remove weathering, 150-grit to smooth.
2. Apply a finish coat:
   - **Boiled linseed oil:** penetrating, matte. Apply thin coat, wipe excess, dry 48 h.
   - **Paste wax:** protective and brings out the natural colour.
   - **Acrylic spray:** quick, durable, available in satin or gloss.
3. Decorate as desired (pyrography, paint, wrapped cordage on handle zone).

## 4. Fill Media Options

| Media | Sound character | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dried amaranth / millet seeds | Soft, dense, long cascade | Lightweight; excellent for slow, rain-like effect |
| Dried rice | Medium density, crisp | Slightly louder; can absorb humidity — seal end caps well |
| Small pebbles (3–5 mm) | Louder, staccato | Good for outdoor performance; durable |
| Glass or wooden beads | Bright, articulate | Most consistent; heaviest; produce the most percussion-like sound |
| Mix (seeds + beads) | Layered texture | Seeds for soft base, beads for articulate top end |

Fill media can be replaced by removing one end cap. Design for removability if experimentation is planned.

---

## 5. Acceptance Criteria

| Check | Pass |
|---|---|
| Cascade sound | Steady rainfall cascade from end to end, ~3–8 s travel time |
| No rattle | End caps and seams produce no loose joint noise |
| No leakage | Shake vigorously — no fill media escapes from end caps or wall cracks |
| Working pith baffle | Sound is noticeably more gradual than a hollow tube would produce |
