# Agave Dust Safety Annex — Raphides & PPE

> **Canonical source.** This is the single authoritative safety reference for agave (and yucca) machining dust in this project. Do **not** duplicate this content elsewhere. Other documents (makerspace A7, build guides, stock-prep SOPs) must **link** to this file, not copy it.

---

## 1. The Hazard: Calcium-Oxalate Raphides

Agave and yucca plants store calcium oxalate in needle-shaped crystals called **raphides**, embedded in the cell walls throughout the stalk, skin, and pith. Raphides are typically 100–400 µm long — invisible to the naked eye — and are released as airborne dust when agave or yucca is:

- Cross-cut with a saw
- Bored, gouged, or drilled
- Sanded or rasped
- Dried-split (splitting also releases fibre dust)

**Why they matter:**

| Exposure route | Effect |
|---|---|
| Skin contact (fine dust) | Contact dermatitis: itching, redness, localised irritation — sometimes delayed by 20–60 min |
| Eye contact | Mechanical irritation, conjunctivitis; crystals can scratch the cornea |
| Inhalation | Upper-respiratory irritation, throat irritation; repeated exposure can cause persistent airway sensitivity |
| Ingestion (rare, via hand-to-mouth) | Oral and GI irritation |

Raphide hazard is distinct from wood dust carcinogenicity — it is a mechanical/chemical irritant, not a known carcinogen. However, it is a real and unpleasant hazard that must be controlled.

Yucca has a comparable raphide load to agave; treat all yucca dust as equivalent.

---

## 2. Required PPE

Wear all three during any agave or yucca machining (cutting, boring, sanding, splitting):

| PPE item | Minimum spec | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Respirator** | N95 particulate respirator (minimum); P100 half-mask preferred for prolonged sanding or gouging | Disposable N95 is adequate for short-duration cuts; P100 for sessions > 15 min or heavy material removal |
| **Eye protection** | Safety glasses with side shields (minimum); full goggles preferred for overhead or power-tool work | Standard prescription glasses are **not** adequate — no side protection |
| **Skin coverage** | Long sleeves; gloves for prolonged hand contact | Nitrile or latex gloves for gouging sessions; bare hands on smooth finished surfaces are generally fine |

**Do not:** work without the respirator even for a "quick" cut. A single cross-cut releases a measurable raphide cloud.

---

## 3. Dust Control Measures

In addition to PPE, use at least one active dust-control method:

| Method | Effectiveness | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shop vacuum with HEPA filter at the cut/bore point | High — captures dust at source | Most effective; hold the vacuum nozzle within 2 in of the work |
| Wet method: mist the work surface before cutting/sanding | Moderate — suppresses airborne fraction | Useful for coarse cutting; do not soak (softens the stalk) |
| Outdoor or well-ventilated area | Low alone — dilution only | Supplement with one of the above; insufficient as a standalone control |
| Dust mask (non-rated cloth mask) | Not adequate | Not a substitute for a rated N95 or P100 |

---

## 4. After-Work Hygiene

1. **Wash hands and forearms** with soap and water before touching your face.
2. **Change or brush off clothing** before moving to a non-dusty area — raphides cling to fabric and can cause delayed contact irritation.
3. **Clean the work area** with a HEPA vacuum (not a leaf blower or compressed air — that re-aerosolises the dust).
4. If skin irritation occurs: rinse the area with cool water for 5–10 min. Antihistamine cream can reduce itching. Seek medical attention if irritation is severe or involves the eyes.

---

## 5. Quick Pre-Work Checklist

```
[ ] Respirator — N95 or P100, fitted and sealed
[ ] Safety glasses or goggles — side shields present
[ ] Long sleeves (and gloves if extended gouging session)
[ ] Dust-control active — HEPA vac at source, or wet method, or outdoors + vac
[ ] Work area clear of unprotected bystanders
```
