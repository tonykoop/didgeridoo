# Knuckle-Tap / Cure-Verification QA Check

A simple acoustic and visual field test to confirm that an agave or yucca stalk is fully cured and structurally ready for instrument work. No tools required. Do this check before grading (see `material-spec.md`) and again immediately before any bore prep.

---

## 1. Knuckle-Tap Sound Test

This is the primary cure indicator.

**How to perform:**
1. Hold the stalk horizontally with one hand near the centre, letting it hang freely (don't grip it tightly — you want it to ring, not be damped).
2. Knock firmly on the outer cortex with a single knuckle at several points: base end, mid-stalk, and upper end.
3. Listen to the sound produced.

| Sound | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear, hollow ring** — bright, resonant knock, slight sustain | Cured: internal moisture has escaped, wall is hard and dry | PASS — proceed to grading |
| **Dull thud** — low, flat, no sustain | Green or under-cured: moisture still present; cells not yet hardened | FAIL — return to drying; recheck in 2–4 weeks |
| **Soft knock, no resonance** — sounds like tapping soft wood | Rot, fungal damage, or collapsed wall section | REJECT — do not use; inspect for visible rot |
| **Crackling or hollow thump at one spot only** — otherwise rings clear | Localised delamination or internal crack | CONDITIONAL — mark the spot; cut planning must avoid this zone |

Repeat at 6-in intervals along the stalk length. The sound should be consistent from base to upper end; abrupt changes indicate a problem zone.

---

## 2. Visual Cues

Supplement the tap test with these visual checks:

| Feature | Cured (pass) | Under-cured or problem (fail/hold) |
|---|---|---|
| Skin colour | Straw, tan, grey-tan, or silvered; uniform | Green patches, dark damp zones, brown wet spots |
| Surface texture | Papery, slightly rough, hard to fingernail scratch | Soft, yielding to fingernail, waxy feel |
| Shrinkage cracks | Fine longitudinal hairline cracks along grain — normal | Cross-grain cracks, spiral cracks, or cracks wider than 1/8 in |
| Cut-end grain | Dry, pale, visible ring structure; hard to the touch | Dark, moist, or soft at the cut face |
| Smell | Faint dusty or plant smell at most | Mildew, sour, or ammonia smell — indicates fungal or rot activity |

---

## 3. End-Grain Moisture Check (Optional Confirmation)

If you have a pin-type wood moisture meter, press the pins into the cut end grain.

| Reading | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| ≤ 12 % MC | Cured — instrument-grade |
| 13–18 % MC | Marginal — extend drying, recheck |
| > 18 % MC | Under-cured — not ready |

Agave and yucca are not wood, so moisture-meter calibration is approximate. Use as a secondary check only, not as the sole pass criterion.

---

## 4. When to Re-Check

- **Harvest to cure:** freshly cut agave or yucca stalks need 4–12 weeks of open-air drying depending on diameter, climate, and species. Larger diameter = longer cure.
- **After field storage:** check again if stalks have been stored outdoors, in humid conditions, or in contact with soil.
- **Before bore prep:** re-run the tap test on the specific cut section just before splitting — a section that passed at harvest may have absorbed moisture during storage.
