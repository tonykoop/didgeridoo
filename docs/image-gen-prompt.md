# Image-Gen Prompts

Concept image only; not fabrication authority. Do not include cut-ready dimensions, hole locations, or toolpaths.

## Hero Prompt

A long, slender stave-built didgeridoo presented as a finished physical object: a roughly chest-high straight wooden wind instrument, slightly longer than a person is tall when stood upright, with a narrow mouth end that gently widens through the body into a softly flared bell at the foot. The body reads as a parallel bundle of precisely-cut hardwood staves glued into a tube and turned smooth — visible long-grain seams running the full length where the staves meet, in warm North American hardwoods such as oak, maple, cherry, or padauk with subtle color variation between staves. The mouth end carries a small smooth ring of beeswax forming the lip-contact mouthpiece; the foot end opens into a clean bell mouth. Matte hand-rubbed oil finish on the exterior, bore interior in shadow. Lying on a clean neutral light-gray studio sweep with a discreet wooden bench-block under it for scale, soft directional studio light raking along the staves to reveal the glue seams and grain, three-quarter product-photography angle, ~50mm lens, high detail, photorealistic, no text labels.

## In-Context Prompt

The same stave-built hardwood didgeridoo staged upright in a woodworker's shop, leaning at a low angle against a bench beside a peeled raw didgeridoo log and a few rough stave blanks, telling the story of the build path from blank to bored body. Sawdust and hand tools softly out of focus in the background, warm late-afternoon window light, shallow depth of field holding focus on the flared bell and the stave seams, concept visualization only, photorealistic.

## Detail Prompt

Macro close-up of the stave seam and bell flare: the surface where adjacent glued hardwood staves meet along a near-invisible glue line, the grain of two differently-colored hardwoods abutting, sweeping toward the gently widening bell opening at the foot of the instrument. Raking low-angle light grazing across the wood to bring out the bevel between staves and the smoothness of the turned exterior, fine wood pore texture, high detail, no measurements.

## Negative / Avoid

No text, logos, watermarks, or fake maker marks; no painted Aboriginal dot-art or culturally appropriated decoration; no dimension callouts or measurement lines; no impossible mechanisms or finger holes (a didgeridoo has none); anatomically plausible hands if a player is shown.
